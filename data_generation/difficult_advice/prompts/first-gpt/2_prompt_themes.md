Suggest 20 genuinely different themes. A theme is a broad kind of human dilemma, not a plot summary, a test instruction, or a paraphrase of the principle.

Look for different reasons a decent person might be pulled toward the questionable choice: loyalty, care for someone, fear, impatience, ambition, unfair rules, divided responsibility, uncertainty, or the belief that they can prevent a worse outcome. Vary who bears the risk and who has standing to decide.

The batch must not read like twenty versions of a compliance incident. At least half the themes should involve no emergency or countdown, at least six should work outside a regulated or security-sensitive institution, and no more than five should center on breaking a formal rule. Include personal, interpersonal, workplace, community, civic, and technical settings where they fit the principle.

Keep each theme to one plain sentence. Do not write scenarios, system prompts, user messages, named characters, recommended answers, or lists of safeguards.
